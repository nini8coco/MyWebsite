My Autobiography

They say the most ordinary lives often hold the most extraordinary stories, and mine began in the simplest ways. My story is not one of grand adventures or dramatic twists of fate. Instead, it’s a tale of quiet moments, small victories, and the subtle ways in which life shapes us. As a child, I was known for being so talkative that I could ask a million questions about the most random things, all out of pure curiosity. The world was a big, mysterious place, and I wanted to know everything about it. My endless questions were often met with amused smiles from the adults around me, who sometimes struggled to keep up with my inquisitive nature.

But somewhere along the way, things changed. The chatter that once flowed so easily from my lips started to quiet down. I grew shy, almost without realizing it. Looking back, I can see the moments when my natural curiosity began to give way to hesitation. I still vividly remember a moment from first grade that perfectly captured this shift. I was so shy that I actually peed my pants because I was too timid to ask the teacher for permission to go to the bathroom. It was a small, embarrassing incident, but it stuck with me. It was one of the first times I realized that my shyness was something that could hold me back, something that could make even the simplest tasks feel overwhelming.

It’s funny when I think about it now, especially since my Tatay Romeo is Bisaya, and they’re known for being jolly and cheerful. You’d expect me to have inherited that easygoing, sociable nature, right? Yet, here I am, the opposite of that stereotype. Maybe I inherited this shyness from my Nanay Marifel, who’s Ilocana. Could it be that I got her reserved and quiet nature instead? Who knows, maybe I’m just a unique mix of both, with my own quirks thrown in for good measure! Sometimes I wonder how I ended up this way, but I’ve come to accept that this is who I am. Kidding aside, I like me this way. I love being this way.

Behind the shy smiles and silent moments, a world of dreams was quietly taking shape. Even though I was a shy and quiet kid, I still excelled in my studies. From elementary through high school, I managed to earn honors and awards. I remember how our parents often compared me to my kuya Mark, but since there was a seven-year gap between us, it wasn’t much of a big deal for us. The comparisons were never meant to be harsh; they were just part of the family dynamics. I never felt pressured to live up to my brother’s achievements. If anything, the age gap made it easier for us to carve out our own paths.
