Despite my shyness, I managed to make some friends along the way, and we stuck together all through junior high. We were a close-knit group, and one of our best memories was when we ended up in the same section. It was like the universe had aligned in our favor, giving us the chance to spend even more time together. But then some of the teachers realized that the 'smart kids' needed to be spread out across different sections, and they planned to move some of us. The idea was to balance the classes, to ensure that each section had its fair share of academically strong students.

When we heard the news, we were heartbroken. The thought of being separated from our friends was unbearable. We cried our eyes out in the classroom, huddled together as if we could somehow hold onto each other through sheer force of will. It was a moment of pure, youthful desperation. But in the end, the teachers felt sorry for us and scrapped the idea. Maybe they saw how much we needed each other, or maybe they just couldn’t bear to see us so upset. Whatever the reason, we got to stay together, and that made those years even more special.

Then the COVID-19 pandemic hit, and with it, we finally parted ways as we headed to different schools for senior high. The pandemic changed everything, didn’t it? One moment, we were planning our future, and the next, the world was turned upside down. For me, it meant choosing to study in a neighboring town, where I didn’t know a single soul. It was a big decision, and it felt like a huge step out of my comfort zone. From being talkative to shy and quiet, I was now stepping into my adventurous phase. This time, I was ready to embrace unfamiliar challenges.

But just as I was gearing up for this new chapter, the pandemic threw another curveball. Luckily, or maybe unluckily, the school implemented asynchronous classes. On one hand, it meant I didn’t have to face the nerve-wracking prospect of meeting new people right away. My shy side could stay tucked away for a little longer. On the other hand, it also meant I lost the opportunity to come out of my shell, to push myself to connect with others. So, I still didn’t get to meet my new classmates—or should I say, they didn’t get to meet their new classmate.
